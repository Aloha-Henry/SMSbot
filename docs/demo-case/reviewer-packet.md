# Reviewer packet — Maria S. demo case

Two reviewers, two jobs. Read `thread.md` first, then `timeline.md`. Do not read `protocol.md`
until you have formed a view of the thread; then read it and say whether knowing the library
changes your view.

Answer in writing. Short is fine. "I would not attach this" is a complete answer if you say why.

---

## A. For the personal-injury attorney: is this useful?

You are the plaintiff's lawyer on this file. It is April 1. You have thirty days of this record.

1. **Would you attach a two-page exhibit built from §1–2 of `timeline.md` to a demand?** If yes,
   what would you cut. If no, what is missing.
2. **What does this record tell you about non-economic damages that your intake notes and the
   medical records would not?** Name the three entries you would quote.
3. **Sit with the two good days** (LOG-0027, LOG-0056). Do they make the record more credible to
   an adjuster or a jury, or do you wish they were not there? Say which and why.
4. **The prior condition** (LOG-0079). You now know about a 2019 ladder fall from your own client's
   record rather than from the defense at deposition. Is that better or worse for you.
5. **The attorney messages** (LOG-0074, LOG-0096). Would you have sent them? Would you have sent
   them in this thread rather than by phone? Does "the good days and the hard ones" read as
   coaching to you?
6. **Cadence.** Mon/Wed/Fri plus post-appointment produced 52 client statements in 30 days. Too
   many, too few, or right? Would your clients keep answering?
7. **The paralegal's Mar 11 message** (LOG-0039) told the client to go to PT. Is that the paralegal's
   call to make in this thread?
8. **What would you pay for this per case**, and does $149 flat change how you would use it?
9. **What is the one thing you would need to see before running it on a live client.**

## B. For the independent litigation reviewer: attack it

You are defense counsel, or a neutral asked to think like one. You have been handed this record in
discovery, either by election or by court order. Assume California Superior Court.

### Context

1. Read every `agent_prompt` in sequence. **Is any question leading?** Point to it. Include P-14
   (LOG-0080) and P-13 (LOG-0067), which are the closest calls.
2. The steer at LOG-0042. Does it read as neutral redirection, or as the firm's tool suppressing a
   topic? What would you argue to a jury about it, and does the fact that LOG-0041 is still in the
   record change the argument?
3. **The disclosure at LOG-0003** says "anything in your file may be used in your case." Does a
   statement made with that knowledge lack the confidentiality §952 requires at the moment of
   communication? Or does it describe counsel's future discretion, like any client telling a lawyer
   facts the lawyer may use? Give your best argument each way.
4. The client knows an AI is asking. Does that change how you would characterize her statements to
   a jury ("she was performing for a machine"), and how would you rebut it?

### Hearsay, California

5. Take **T-23** (groceries, LOG-0052) and **T-30** (recital, LOG-0069). Under §1241, is a statement
   made minutes to an hour after the conduct a "contemporaneous statement"? Under §1250, what part
   of each entry is a then-existing state and what part is past fact? Is the label in `timeline.md`
   right?
6. Take **T-12** (LOG-0035, "he said lumbar strain…"). Confirm this is double hearsay under §1201
   and say what the plaintiff should do with it.
7. **§1271 for the log.** What foundation would you demand from the platform custodian, and where
   would you attack trustworthiness (agent involvement in creating the record; the firm's interest;
   the "mode of preparation")? Does it matter that 42 of 42 agent prompts are from a fixed library?
8. If you are the defense, which client statements do you offer under §1220, and does offering
   them open anything under §356?

### Unfavorable entries

9. Assume the plaintiff attaches an exhibit built from §2 of `timeline.md` to a demand and later
   produces the full record. Which entries in §6 of `timeline.md` do you use, and how much do they
   hurt?
10. The back series is flat across thirty days (6 → 5). The shoulder improves (5 → 3). How would
    you use the shoulder line?
11. LOG-0093: the client told your adjuster she was "fine." Now you have the full thread showing
    "5" the same day. Which of you does that help?

### Disclosure scope

12. The firm elects to produce the **client statements and agent prompts** (LOG rows of those
    classes) but withholds LOG-0039, 0065, 0074, 0096 (attorney and staff messages) as privileged,
    and withholds `timeline.md` §3–6 as work product. Under §912(a) and §356, what do you move to
    compel, and what is your best argument that the election reached more than the firm intended?
13. Does the paralegal's message LOG-0039 (go to PT; call me about closer options) look like a
    privileged communication, a business communication, or a mixed one?
14. Is the **attorney's disposition on INT-06** ("not a credibility problem on its face") opinion
    work product under CCP §2018.030(a), qualified work product under (b), or neither, given it was
    written in a vendor's system in response to a vendor's flag?
15. If the court finds the agent prompts and client statements **not privileged** (say, because the
    AI is not "reasonably necessary" under §952), does that finding reach LOG-0074 and LOG-0096?
    Does it reach the client's emails to the firm? Say why or why not, with authority if you have it.

### The one question that matters

16. **Is a thread like this one more dangerous to the plaintiff than having no record at all?**
    Answer for this thread specifically, not in the abstract, and name the entries that drive your
    answer.

or the questions we send to California ethics and evidence counsel. Nothing in the answers is
expected to be comfortable. The point of the exercise is the list of things to fix.
