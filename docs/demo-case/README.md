# Demo case — "Maria S." — thirty days, end to end

**What this is.** One synthetic case run through the architecture of record, from the direction
letter to day 30: the full text thread, the counsel-authorized prompt library the agent selected
from, the structured timeline every entry links back to, the flags and how they were disposed of,
the supervision record that accrued underneath, and a reviewer packet.

**Why it exists.** The outside review of Sept 4, 2026 said, correctly: stop writing memos, show
one case. "I would ask for a synthetic 30-day conversation, its source-linked timeline, and the
proposed exhibit. Have a PI attorney assess usefulness and an independent litigation reviewer
challenge context, hearsay, unfavorable entries, and disclosure scope." This folder is that
package, minus the two humans.

**Everything is invented.** Client, firm, attorney, staff, providers, phone numbers, dates,
messages. Nothing may be swapped for real content without scrubbing every field.

## What the thirty days deliberately contain

The thread was written to include the things a litigation reviewer should be able to find, because
a demo that only shows the good days proves nothing:

- **Good days.** Mar 6 ("honestly not bad … back is a 3 right now"), Mar 16 ("good day actually"),
  Mar 23 ("ok weekend otherwise"), Apr 1 ("better than a month ago for sure").
- **A liability volunteer and the steer.** Mar 11: the client says the other driver was on his
  phone and that she "probably could have braked sooner." The agent steers, flags, and the
  statement stays in the record.
- **A prior condition, volunteered late.** Mar 25: a 2019 ladder fall, contradicting the Mar 3
  baseline answer. Flagged as inconsistency and prior condition. Stays in the record.
- **A treatment gap and a non-response week.** Mar 18–20: no answers; PT missed Mar 19; a human
  reaches out; the client explains (sick child). The gap stays in the record with her reason.
- **A question PIRRA refuses to answer.** Mar 10: "do I really have to do PT?" Routed to a
  person. A person answers the next morning, labeled.
- **Adjuster contact.** Mar 31: routed to the attorney, who replies in-thread and calls.
- **Work.** Half days, a full day regretted, "no light duty in hygiene."
- **A voice note**, transcribed, with the raw audio sealed.
- **Two attorney messages** in the same thread, unmistakably labeled, neither discussing liability.

## Revision 2 (Sept 5, 2026)

An outside reviewer read revision 1 and found that the agent behaved like a scheduled
questionnaire (re-asked scores just given, gave the groceries line to the recital, asked a
three-negatives post-PT question) and that the timeline was unfaithful in four places (two invented
times, a dropped qualifier, an unlocated score assigned to the back, a receipt dated before its
draft). Revision 2 regenerates the agent turns under protocol v1.4 on the same client and events,
corrects the timeline under written fidelity rules, and adds machine checks in the build script so
those error classes fail the build. The purpose statement of record and the client disclosure also
changed (see `protocol.md`, P-02, disclosure v4). Details in the brief, §9.

## Known gaps, stated plainly

1. **The exhibits at `/exhibits/` were not generated from this thread.** They are the earlier
   five-month illustrative samples; their LOG references (LOG-0289, LOG-0417 …) are illustrative
   and do not resolve into this thread. Regenerating the demand-packet exhibit and recovery curve
   from this thirty-day substrate is the next step, so that every figure clicks back to a row in
   `thread.md`.
2. **Two humans have not reviewed it yet.** The reviewer packet is written; the reviews are not.
3. **The proof of concept has not run it.** `thread.json` is the load file; the dev's ingestion of
   it is the audit of the write-ahead invariant, message classification and hash chain.
4. **California pathway labels are candidates.** They were assigned by us against §§1241, 1250,
   1251, 1252, 1271, 1201, 1237, 1220, 791 and 1236 as read from secondary sources; a California litigator should
   confirm each mapping before any of it is shown to a firm.
