One record. Three surfaces. One decision that releases anything.

The client sees a text thread. The firm sees a workbench. Nothing leaves the protected workflow until the attorney decides whether and how to use it. Every picture below is the same system from a different distance.

Purpose of record. Collect information confidentially within the attorney-client relationship. Prepare materials privately for counsel. Require an authorized disclosure decision before anything is released outside that protected workflow. It is the attorney's decision whether to use the exhibits and how.
WHO

The client

Texts the firm's number. An AI assistant, disclosed as such and directed by her named attorney, asks how she is doing. She answers in her own words, on her own schedule.

WHAT PIRRA DOES

Captures and structures

Writes every message to an append-only, hash-chained log before anything reads it. Structures the record with a link back to every source message. Raises flags. Never discusses liability, never answers legal or medical questions, never talks to anyone but the client.

WHO DECIDES

The firm

The paralegal works the queue. The attorney directs in two minutes, supervises from a phone, and is the only person who can release anything: an exhibit, a production, a certification.

THE RULES THAT DO NOT BEND

What every picture above assumes

  1. Preserves evidence that would otherwise be lost. Never creates it. Neutral questions, adverse entries kept.
  2. No privilege or admissibility guarantees, anywhere. The architecture is describable; outcomes are counsel's to argue.
  3. AI status always disclosed, and the attorney named in the first message.
  4. Damages only. Never liability. Fault, speed, scene and insurance are steered to the attorney, flagged, and kept.
  5. Written before read. No model sees a message that is not already in the firm's record.
  6. The AI composes nothing at the default level. It selects from a counsel-authorized library and may quote the client's own words back, verified verbatim.
  7. Nothing is deleted. Corrections annotate. Flags that turn out to be nothing stay as "we looked."
  8. One decision releases anything, and it belongs to the attorney, over the whole record.
