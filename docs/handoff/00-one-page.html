WHAT COUNSEL SAW

Monday's digest, on her phone: "Sat: missed daughter's recital, 'couldn't sit that long.' Back 6, spasming. Sitting 'about the same' since." Opening it logged her review. Nothing else was asked of her.

In the timeline, two entries she can trust:

T-30 · event: Sat, time not stated · reported 16:20 · missed recital; could not sit; left; "cried in the car" · source LOG-0071
T-31 · same event · reported 16:29 · ~15 min seated, spasm, left for car · "6 right now, still spasming" · source LOG-0073 · candidate pathway: present sensation (CA Evid. Code §1250), for counsel's review

In the exhibit, if counsel prepares one: the verbatim excerpt with its log reference and a point on the recovery curve. What did not happen: no question about the crash, no suggestion of what to report, no reading her scores back, no comment on her case.

WHY FIRMS ARE ASKING FOR THIS

Non-economic loss is often the largest and least-documented part of a recovery. Medical records show treatment, not the recital or the car seat. Clients rarely keep journals, and a self-kept diary is open to attack as curated. A record built with neutral questions, good days included, is harder to attack.

Opposing counsel now asks about AI in discovery. Federal courts split in 2026 on whether a litigant's AI conversations are protected; the case that lost involved a consumer tool with no attorney direction. PIRRA is attorney-directed from the first message and built to stand up if produced.
