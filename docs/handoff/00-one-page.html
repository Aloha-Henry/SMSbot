The pain journal your clients will actually keep, built as evidence from the first message.

PIRRA is an SMS-native, attorney-directed AI that captures a personal-injury client's recovery in their own words during the months between intake and resolution, and turns it into court-ready exhibits where every figure clicks back to the text message it came from. The client sees a text thread. The firm sees a workbench. The court sees a PDF. One flat price per case.

WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

A disclosed AI assistant on the firm's number, directed by the named attorney, checks in a few times a week and after appointments: how are you right now, what can't you do today. The client answers by text or voice. No app, no login.

An append-only, hash-chained, independently timestamped record where every message is classified at write and every derived fact links to its source. Neutral questions from a counsel-approved library. Good days kept. Liability content steered to the attorney and never deleted.

Exhibits on demand: the two-page demand-packet exhibit, the recovery-curve summary chart, the depo-prep log, and a custodian certification in the form the forum accepts. Released only when the attorney elects, and only as a whole record.

WHY FIRMS NEED IT

Non-economic damages are the majority of settlement value and the least-documented part of every file. The months between intake and demand produce nothing, and the demand is reconstructed from memory and medical records.

Clients don't keep journals. The few who do hand the firm an uncontrolled diary the defense can subpoena and cherry-pick.

Defense counsel now asks for AI use in discovery. Federal courts split in 2026 on whether a litigant's AI conversations are protected; the loser used a consumer tool with no attorney direction. Firms need a record attorney-directed by design and survivable if produced.

California is writing AI duties into the Rules of Professional Conduct. Firms need supervision that happens by default, with a record to show for it.

HOW IT IS DIFFERENT

Everyone else builds a communication tool or a case manager and calls its byproduct a record. PIRRA builds the record as evidence from message one and lets the attorney decide whether it is ever seen.

The question asked. A case manager asks "did you go to PT." PIRRA asks the damages question, in the present tense, at a density the evidence rules reward.

The output. Nobody else produces a document a firm attaches to a demand or takes to mediation, with provenance to the message and a custodian certification behind it.

The architecture. The client-facing AI reads the thread and nothing else: no case file, no tools, no case-status answers. Two planes: the record, and the work product prepared for counsel. One gate out, and the attorney holds it.

Supervision as a product. Autonomy levels elected per case, receipts logged by reading, three-tap dispositions, a one-click Rule 5.3 record.

A case manager's log is not an exhibit. A treatment timeline exported from a chat thread that also answered case-status questions is the uncontrolled diary with a logo on it, because the record was not designed as evidence from the first message and cannot be retrofitted.

Where we are. Architecture of record complete after three blind review rounds and two outside legal critiques. Prototypes live. A thirty-day synthetic case exists end to end with thread, source-linked timeline, flags and supervision record. Proof of concept built; build spec written against the rules. Next: a PI attorney and an adversarial reviewer on the demo case, California counsel on the open questions, then a synthetic-data pilot.

What we will not say. We do not promise privilege or admissibility, anywhere. The record is built so that if produced it is accurate and complete, and the attorney, not the software, decides what is released. We preserve evidence that would otherwise be lost; we do not create it. The AI never pretends to be a person, never discusses fault, never talks to anyone but the client.
