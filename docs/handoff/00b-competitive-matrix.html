Where the difference actually is

  1. The purpose of the conversation. The other agents are built for adherence and staff capacity, and collect symptoms on the way. PIRRA's conversation exists to capture the client's account for counsel, and its questions, memory and pacing are designed for that.
  2. The output. We have not found another product whose deliverable is an exhibit with provenance to the message and a custodian certification behind it. That is a statement about what we found, not proof of absence.
  3. Record design that has to be true from message one. Neutral questions, adverse entries kept, classification at write, an append-only chain, and an assistant that cannot see the case file. These are design decisions, not features, and they are hard to add to a product whose agent already answers case questions in the same thread.
  4. Disclosure control and supervision as product behavior. We describe what the software does (nothing leaves without the attorney's decision; the supervision record accrues by default) and do not describe legal outcomes.
